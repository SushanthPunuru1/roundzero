The change log is the least exciting habit in competition and the one that
most reliably saves a round. It costs a few seconds per change and pays for
itself the first time your score goes down and nobody knows why.

## Why a score can drop

Points do not just fail to arrive — they get taken away. Stopping a service
the README requires, breaking a config file, locking out an account that
should work: all of these show up as a score *decrease*, sometimes twenty
minutes after the change that caused it.

At that moment you have one question: what did we just do? Without a log, the
answer is somewhere among everything three people have touched in the last
hour. With one, it is the last four entries.

## What an entry needs

Three things:

```
14:32  disabled telnet service              — Sam
14:35  set PermitRootLogin no, restarted ssh — Sam
14:41  removed user "backdoor"              — Alex
14:44  set password policy minlen 14        — Sam
```

Time, what changed, who did it. That is enough to reverse anything and enough
to tell two people's work apart. A shared doc, a text file, or paper all work
— what matters is that everyone writes to the same one.

## Log the change, not the intention

Write what you actually did, in enough detail to undo it. "Fixed SSH" is
useless three hours later. "Set PermitRootLogin no in sshd_config, restarted
ssh" can be reversed by anyone on the team without asking you.

If you edited a file, note which file. If you changed a setting, note the old
value as well when you have it.

## It pairs with backups

The log tells you *what* you changed; a backup lets you put it back:

```
sudo cp /etc/ssh/sshd_config /etc/ssh/sshd_config.bak
```

Back up before you edit, log after you change. Together they turn a broken
service into a thirty-second revert instead of a rebuild.

## With a team, it becomes essential

One person can sometimes hold their changes in their head. Two people on one
machine cannot. Without a shared log you get silent overlap — one person
undoing another's work, or both making the same change and neither knowing
whether it took.

The log is what makes parallel work visible. It is the cheapest coordination
tool you have, and unlike everything else in a round, it costs nothing to
maintain.
